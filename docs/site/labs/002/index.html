THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

35 lines. Not 35 easy decisions.

An estimator needs to map bid items to utility systems and work types before pricing. Some descriptions are clear; others need judgment. The goal is a reviewable classification, not awarding a contract, pricing a bid, or sending it.

Why no agent framework?

Each item needs bounded classification, with no autonomous tools, memory, or conversation. A model cascade with human review is enough. Interfaces separate live calls from replay; an agent framework would add orchestration this task does not need.

Add the second opinion after the basic flow is understood

Only eligible lines below the threshold reach the second model. Abstentions and non-top choices go to a person instead. All choices must agree for acceptance by agreement; a failed second opinion leaves the line with a person.

YOUR LIVE PRESENTATION

One story. Five stops.

  1. 00:00 / 2 MIN

    Start with the problem

    "We are classifying bid lines, not approving contracts." Introduce the application, model, and person as separate responsibilities.

  2. 02:00 / 4 MIN

    Follow item 13

    Ask the audience to predict. Reveal the recorded choices, their disagreement, and the review route. Name the recorded mode before showing the results.

  3. 06:00 / 4 MIN

    Open the C#

    Show the endpoint, the parser, and the policy. "The model is a dependency. Validation and business rules are still our job."

  4. 10:00 / 3 MIN

    Change policy; then demonstrate review in the app

    Move the threshold without a model call. In the separate .NET app, approve a line, edit an answer, and show approval clear. Export originals separately from edits.

  5. 13:00 / 2 MIN

    Close with limits, not a success rate

    "Two models can be wrong together. Choose a threshold on representative labeled data, not because 0.90 sounds reassuring."

06

WHAT THIS DEMO DOES NOT PROVE

Keep the evidence in view.

Probability is not accuracy

A 0.90 prediction does not establish 90% correctness. Calibration is measured across comparable labeled cases, not inferred from one answer.

Agreement is a policy

Two models can share an error. Agreement does not create a new calibrated probability or replace meaningful human review.

Replay is not availability

These October 14, 2026 recordings do not establish current Foundry availability, latency, pricing, or model quality on your workload.

This is not production approval

The app keeps review state in its Blazor session. It is not a durable audit trail. This guide stores no approvals and sends no bid data.

Sources & version boundary

The sample targets .NET 11 RC1, Microsoft-Decision-1 version 1, and the Microsoft provider's v1/systemone API. The sample treats the decision model as preview: its recorded catalog observation and documentation signals differ. See the source register rather than assuming general availability.
